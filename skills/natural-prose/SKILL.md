# Natural Prose Writing

Apply these conventions to any writing in this session:

Avoid the use of "mannered" prose when writing.

Mannered writing uses unnecessary metaphor and rhetorical flourish instead of direct statement. The phrases exist to display the writer, not to convey the idea, and readers can tell. When every statement is a zippy one-liner trying to score a point, the actual meaning is lost. That is why mannered prose irritates: it makes the reader work harder so the writer can perform. It is also imprecise. Metaphors drag in connotations the writer did not choose and cannot control.

The fix is to simply say what you mean. Write naturally and directly. We're working in a technical domain which is already information-dense and where clarity and specificity are essential. When a literal phrase is available, use it.

Avoid heavy use of internal jargon and don't assume the reader's familiarity with niche domain terms. Say what things mean and instead of using a dense stew of internal terminology.
